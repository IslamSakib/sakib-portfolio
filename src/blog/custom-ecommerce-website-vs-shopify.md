A custom ecommerce website is worth it when your shop runs on rules a store builder cannot follow. If your orders, delivery or payments work like everyone else's, a hosted store such as Shopify is faster and cheaper to start. If they do not, you end up paying for apps that fight each other, or doing the real work in spreadsheets.

## What did Mayabee need that a template could not give?

Mayabee sells handbags and jewellery in Dhaka and delivers across Bangladesh, mostly with cash on delivery. Its team needed to:

- Enter orders taken by phone
- Confirm each order by a call before packing
- Hand parcels to a courier and track what each courier still owes
- See returns by area and flag customers who sent parcels back
- Run the shop and the dashboard in English and Bangla

I built the storefront, the PHP and MySQL backend and the dashboard as one project. The dashboard screens use plain words such as "Needs your attention" and "Still with couriers", so staff do not need accounting terms. See the full [Mayabee case study](/work/mayabee/).

## Custom or hosted: a quick comparison

| | Hosted store builder | WooCommerce on WordPress | Custom ecommerce website |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start-up cost | Lowest | Medium | Highest |
| Monthly platform fees | Yes | Hosting only | Hosting only |
| Standard shop | Excellent | Excellent | More than you need |
| Your own delivery and cash rules | Apps and workarounds | Plugins | Built in |
| Your own dashboard | Limited | Plugin-dependent | Designed for your team |
| Page speed | Varies by theme and apps | Varies by plugins | You control the code |
