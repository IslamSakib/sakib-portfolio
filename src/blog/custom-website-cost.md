Custom website cost follows the work, not the page count. Six things move the price: how much design it needs, how many different page types it has, whether people sign in, whether it takes payments, how many other systems it connects to, and whether it needs an admin dashboard for your team. Everything else is small by comparison.

## The six things that set the price

1. **Design.** A site with its own UI and UX design costs more than one built on a bought theme, and it fits your business better. I design the screens myself, so design and build are one job.
2. **Page types, not pages.** Fifty product pages from one template cost little. Five different layouts cost more.
3. **Accounts and roles.** Sign-in, passwords, email confirmation and different staff permissions add real work. Mayabee has four roles and a confirmation email for new devices.
4. **Payments.** Cash on delivery is simple. Cards, wallets and subscriptions need gateways, testing and failure handling.
5. **Integrations.** Each connection to a CRM, courier, accounting tool or email service is its own piece of work.
6. **The admin dashboard.** Orders, stock, customers and reports are often half the project.

## How to get a fair quote

Send the developer these five things and you will get a more accurate price in less time:

- Your competitors or sites you like
- The one thing visitors or staff must be able to do
- Who needs to sign in, and what each person can see
- Payments and tools it must connect to
- A launch date, if there is a real one

A good quote lists what is included, what is not, the number of revision rounds and who owns the code. You should own the code, the design files and every login.
