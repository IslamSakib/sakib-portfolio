The best WordPress maintenance services aren't the cheapest or the biggest. They're the ones where updates get tested before they hit your live site, backups sit somewhere other than your server, and a real person replies when your site breaks on a Friday evening. Below I compare five services on exactly that, with prices taken from their own pricing pages on 16 October 2026. One of them is mine, so I've stuck to what each provider publishes and let you judge.

## How I compared them

You probably care about four things, so those are the columns:

- **Price per month**, as published on the provider's own site.
- **Developer time**: how much real work you get beyond updates and backups.
- **How updates are handled**: tested first, or switched on and hoped for the best.
- **Who answers**: a support desk, a shared queue or one named person.

Prices change, so check each provider's page before you decide. I've linked every one in the sources below.

## What each one does well, and what to check

### WP Site Plan: the lowest entry price

At $59 a month, WP Site Plan's Essentials plan covers backups, updates, a firewall and malware scans and removal. Edits only start on the $119 plan, as help-desk tasks of roughly 30 minutes each, handled Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm Eastern, within one to two business days. **Check:** if your site breaks at night or on a weekend, that window matters.

### WP Buffs: the big team with 24/7 emergency support

WP Buffs starts at $89 a month with weekly updates, cloud backups four times a day and 24/7 emergency support. "Unlimited edits" start at $179 and exclude custom development. **Check:** ask what counts as one edit, and who does the work once your site needs code rather than content changes.

### Pronto Marketing: maintenance that includes hosting

Pronto's $99 Technical Support plan bundles hosting, backups, security, plugin updates and a hack-fix guarantee, with a one-business-day turnaround. Unlimited edits and projects start at $299. **Check:** because hosting is included, you'd move your site to them, which suits some businesses and is a hassle for others.

### FatLab Web Support: fixed hours, spelled out

FatLab is clear about hours: none on the $99 plan, then 1, 3 or 8 hours a month on the higher plans, with extra hours at $150 and weekly updates on all of them. **Check:** at $199 for one hour a month, the hours cost more than most, but you know exactly what you're getting.

### Me: one developer who already knows your site

My two plans are $149 (1 hour of changes a month) and $299 (3 hours plus a monthly mobile speed check). Every update is tested before it reaches your live site, backups are kept off your server daily, and urgent problems get a reply within 24 hours. **Check:** I work alone. You get the same person every time, not a team that covers every hour of the night. If you need someone awake at 3am every day of the year, a larger company fits better.

## Which kind of service fits your site?

**Your site is simple and rarely changes.** Pick the cheapest plan that tests updates and keeps backups off your server. WP Site Plan's entry plan or a Care plan like mine both cover it.

**Your team changes the site every week.** Look at developer time first. A plan with real hours (FatLab's, or my Care + development) or a well-run help desk saves you more than a cheaper plan you outgrow.

**You run a store.** Make sure checkout and payments are tested after every update. Most providers quote stores separately, me included.

**You want to change host anyway.** A hosting-plus-maintenance plan like Pronto's means one bill and one company to call.

## Red flags in any maintenance plan

- **Updates switched on automatically with no testing.** That's what a free plugin does; you're paying for someone to check.
- **Backups stored on the same server.** If the server fails or is hacked, the backups go with it.
- **"Unlimited" with no task size or queue time.** Ask for both in writing.
- **No named reply time.** "Fast support" isn't a promise; "within 24 hours" is.
- **You don't get the logins.** You should own every account, always.

## Questions to ask before you sign up

1. Are updates tested on a copy of my site before they go live?
2. Where are my backups kept, and how long?
3. How many developer hours do I get, and what does extra time cost?
4. How fast will you reply if the site goes down, and on which days?
5. Is malware removal included, or charged on top?
6. Do I keep every login if I leave?
