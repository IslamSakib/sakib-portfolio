Website maintenance cost depends on three things: how many parts the site has that need updating, how much it earns or loses when it is down, and how many hours of changes you want each month. Most business sites are well served by a plan at $149 to $299 a month.

## What a monthly plan covers

1. **Updates, tested first.** The platform, themes, plugins and libraries are updated on a copy of the site, checked, then released. The test is what you pay for, not the click.
2. **Backups kept off the server.** A backup on the same server dies with the server.
3. **Monitoring.** Someone is told when the site goes down or a page breaks, before a customer is.
4. **Hours of changes.** Small edits, fixes and tweaks, so you are not billed for every email.
5. **A plain-English report.** What was updated, what was found, what is next.

## How the price compares

| Option | What you pay | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly plan | $149 or $299 a month | Updates, backups, monitoring, changes, one developer who knows the site |
| Pay per fix | $50 and up per repair | A fix after something breaks, with no protection before |
| Do nothing | $0 until it breaks | Outdated software, no backups, a bigger bill later |

Extra developer time on an existing project is $25 an hour. All prices are in US dollars.

## What pushes the cost up

- Many plugins or third-party services, because each needs testing after updates.
- An online store, because the checkout must be tested after every update.
- Several sites on one account.
- A site already hacked or broken, which needs a repair before a plan starts.

## How to compare quotes

Ask what is tested before an update goes live, where backups are kept, how fast urgent problems get a reply and who owns the logins. A low price that skips the testing is not cheaper, only later.
