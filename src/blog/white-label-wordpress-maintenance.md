Your agency launches sites, and then the clients email you when a plugin breaks something. WordPress maintenance for agencies usually means white label: you offer monthly care plans to your clients without doing the maintenance in-house. A partner keeps the sites updated, backed up and working. You sell the plan under your own name and stay the client's only contact. For design and marketing agencies, it's one of the simplest ways to turn one-off builds into steady monthly revenue. Here's how it works and how to set it up well.

## Why should my agency sell maintenance?

Every site you launch needs looking after. If you don't offer it, your client will either neglect the site, which reflects badly on your work when it breaks, or hire someone else, who may also pick up their next redesign. Reselling maintenance:

- **Creates recurring revenue** from clients you already have
- **Keeps you in contact** between projects, which is where new work comes from
- **Protects your work:** the site you built stays fast and secure
- **Costs less than hiring** someone to maintain sites full time

## How does white label maintenance work?

Your client only ever deals with you:

1. **You sell the plan** to your client under your agency's brand and price.
2. **The partner gets access** to the site through their own user account, which you or your client can remove at any time.
3. **The partner does the work:** updates tested on staging, backups, monitoring, fixes and small changes.
4. **Reports go out under your brand,** written so a non-technical client understands them.
5. **Your client contacts you** with requests. You pass them on, or the partner replies through your helpdesk under your name.

The client never sees the partner's name.

## What should my care plan include?

Whatever you charge, include the basics that actually protect the site:

- **Tested updates** to WordPress, the theme and plugins, applied on staging first
- **Daily backups stored off the server,** with restores tested
- **Uptime and security monitoring**
- **A set number of hours** each month for small changes
- **A stated reply time** for urgent problems
- **A monthly report** under your brand

My guide to [what WordPress maintenance includes](/blog/what-wordpress-maintenance-includes/) goes through each part, and the [WordPress maintenance checklist](/blog/wordpress-maintenance-checklist/) shows the tasks behind it.

## How do I price care plans for my clients?

Price by what the client gets, not by what the partner charges you. A useful structure is two or three tiers:

- **Essential:** updates, backups, monitoring and a report
- **Growth:** the same plus a few hours of changes each month and a speed check
- **Store:** extra testing for WooCommerce, payment checks and faster response

Published 2026 price guides put agency maintenance plans at roughly $200 to over $1,000 a month, depending on what's included (see [WordPress maintenance cost](/blog/wordpress-maintenance-cost/)). Your margin covers account management, client communication and the risk you carry as the name on the contract.

## How do I choose a maintenance partner?

If you'd rather not build this in-house, I run [white label WordPress support](/white-label-wordpress-maintenance/) for agencies. Either way, look for the same things you'd want if you were the client:

- **Updates are tested,** not just switched to automatic
- **Backups are off-site** and restores are tested
- **Clear hours and reply times,** not "unlimited"
- **Confidentiality:** an NDA, no client contact, no partner branding anywhere
- **Reports you can rebrand** or that are written under your name
- **Experience with your kind of sites:** if you build stores, the partner must know WooCommerce

## How do I bring a client site onto a plan?

Before the first month, the partner should review each site: WordPress and plugin versions, the theme, hosting and PHP version, existing backups, security status and speed. Expect a short list of problems found and what to fix first. Some sites need a clean-up before they can be maintained properly, such as replacing abandoned plugins or fixing a broken backup. Quote that separately so the monthly price stays predictable.

## What should I watch out for when I resell?

These are where white label plans quietly fall short:

- **Automatic updates sold as maintenance.** If nothing is tested, the first broken update lands on your client.
- **Partner branding leaking through:** plugin author names, admin usernames, email footers. Check each site after onboarding.
- **Unclear scope.** Write down what counts as maintenance and what is a new project.
- **No exit plan.** Make sure all access can be handed back, and that your client owns their hosting, domain and licences.

## What does a month look like behind the scenes?

Here's a typical month of white label maintenance on one client site:

- **Week 1:** updates to WordPress, the theme and plugins tested on staging, then applied live; contact form and key pages checked.
- **Week 2:** backup restore spot-checked; security scan reviewed; one client request handled, such as a new team member page.
- **Week 3:** mobile speed checked on the home page and top landing pages; one plugin with a reported vulnerability updated the same day it was fixed.
- **Week 4:** monthly report written in plain English, under your agency's name, with one recommendation that needs the client's decision.

Your client sees the report and the finished change. The rest happens without them having to think about it, which is exactly what they're paying for.

## How do I sell care plans to my clients?

The easiest moment is at launch, when the client has just seen how much work went into the site. Include a care plan in every build proposal, with a short explanation of what happens without one: plugins fall behind, backups stop, and the first sign of trouble is often a customer email. Clients who decline at launch often come back after their first broken update, so mention the plan again in your handover email.

Describe plans by outcome, not task. "Your site stays fast, secure and up to date" is easier to buy than "plugin updates and database optimisation".

## How do I move my existing client sites over?

If clients already have sites maintained elsewhere, or not maintained at all, move them over one at a time. Start with the site you worry about most. A careful first review often finds outdated plugins, failed backups or a missing SSL renewal, and fixing those quickly is a good way to show the client why the plan is worth paying for.

## Where do I start?

You don't need to move every client at once. Put one site on the plan, see the first two monthly reports and how requests are handled, then add more. It keeps the risk small for you and gives you real examples to show other clients.
