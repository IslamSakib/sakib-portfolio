Custom website vs WordPress is easy to settle once you look at the job. Choose WordPress when your site is mostly content and your team wants to edit pages themselves. Choose a custom website when the site is part of how your business runs: orders with their own rules, staff roles, dashboards, customer accounts. Most arguments go wrong because people compare the platforms instead of the job.

## What is WordPress good at?

WordPress runs about 39.6% of all websites and 58.3% of the sites whose content system is known ([W3Techs, 22 Oct 2026](https://w3techs.com/technologies/details/cm-wordpress)). That is a lot of themes, plugins and developers who know it. It is the right choice for:

- Brochure sites, blogs and company sites your team edits without a developer
- Standard stores built on WooCommerce
- Courses and memberships on plugins such as LearnDash

I build custom WordPress themes and plugins for these jobs. For Moneybag, a licensed payment gateway, that meant a custom payment plugin with OTP sign-in and a merchant onboarding flow.

## When does a custom website win?

A custom build wins when you keep hitting the edge of what a theme and plugins can do:

| Your business needs | WordPress | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Pages your team edits | Strong fit | Needs an admin built for it |
| Cash on delivery by area, courier payouts, return rates | Plugin stack, fragile | Built into the dashboard |
| Staff roles with different permissions | Possible, plugin-dependent | Designed from the start |
| Customer portal with their own data | Heavy | Natural fit |
| A product people sign in to | Wrong tool | Right tool |

Mayabee is the example. It is a shop with an order dashboard, phone orders, courier tracking and cash-on-delivery reports in English and Bangla. On a mobile test run on 23 October 2026, its home page scored 100 for Performance on [Google PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/analysis/https-mayabeebd-com/2fzjuyqshd?form_factor=mobile), with the largest content on screen in 0.9 seconds.

## What does each cost to change later?

A WordPress site changes cheaply when the change fits a plugin, and expensively when you bend a plugin to do something it was not made for. A custom site costs more at the start and changes in the direction you need, because the code was written for your rules. WordPress builds with me start at $300. Custom projects are quoted after I read the brief.

## A quick way to decide

Answer these three questions:

1. Will your team edit pages and posts daily? If yes, lean WordPress.
2. Does the site need rules or screens no plugin provides? If yes, lean custom.
3. Will customers or staff sign in to see their own data? If yes, lean custom.
