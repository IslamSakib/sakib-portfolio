WordPress maintenance is four jobs done on a schedule: updates, backups, security monitoring and a report. Each one prevents a specific way a WordPress site breaks. Here is what each job is, what it protects you from, and what to ask anyone who offers to do it for you.

## Updates, tested before they go live

WordPress itself, your theme and every plugin release updates. Many fix security holes; some add features; a few change how things work. Ignoring updates leaves known holes open. Applying them blindly is how sites break: a plugin update changes something another plugin relied on, and a page turns white.

A careful update runs on a copy of the site first. If the copy still works, the same updates go to the live site. If something breaks, it gets fixed on the copy, and your visitors never see it.

What this prevents: hacks through outdated plugins, and the broken page after an untested update.

## Backups, kept away from the server

A backup stored on the same server as the site disappears with the server. Good backups run every day, are kept somewhere else, and are tested now and then by actually restoring one.

What this prevents: losing the site, or weeks of orders and posts, after a bad update, a hack or a hosting failure. With a recent off-site backup, any of those costs a few hours instead.

## Security and uptime monitoring

Monitoring watches for the site going offline, for files that change when nobody changed them, and for repeated failed logins. When one of those happens, someone is told within minutes rather than finding out from a customer.

What this prevents: a site that has been down or hacked for days before anyone notices.

## A report you can read

Every month you should get a short report in plain language: what was updated, what was found, what was fixed, and anything that needs a decision from you. If you cannot tell from the report what happened to your site, the report is not doing its job.

## What maintenance does not fix

Maintenance keeps a healthy site healthy. It does not repair a site that is already slow or unstable. On TubeOnAI, files left behind by old cache plugins kept clashing with the host's own speed plugin, and the site kept going down. That needed a one-off fix at the cause first: [the TubeOnAI case study](/work/tubeonai/) shows what that involved. Regular checks after that are what stop it coming back.

## What to ask a maintenance provider

- Are updates tested on a copy of the site before they go live?
- Where are backups stored, how often, and when was one last restored?
- How quickly do you reply when the site is down?
- Do I keep every login, and do I own everything if we stop working together?
- What is included in the monthly fee, and what is charged on top?

If the answers are vague, the work probably is too.
