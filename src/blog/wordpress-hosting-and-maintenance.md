WordPress hosting and maintenance are two different jobs, and most site owners pay for one thinking it covers the other. Your host keeps the server running. Maintenance keeps your WordPress site, with its theme and its twenty-odd plugins, working on that server. Here's what each one actually covers, what falls through the gap between them, and when it makes sense to buy them together.

## What your WordPress host looks after

Your host is responsible for the machine your site lives on:

- **The server:** keeping it switched on, patched and fast enough.
- **Uptime at server level:** if the server goes down, that's on them.
- **Server security:** firewalls and isolation from other sites on the same machine.
- **Basic backups:** many hosts take daily backups, usually stored with them.
- **Managed extras:** on managed WordPress hosting, things like automatic core updates and built-in caching.

## What your host usually won't do

This is the gap where most sites break:

- **Test plugin and theme updates** against your site before they go live.
- **Fix a plugin conflict** or a [white screen after an update](/blog/wordpress-update-broke-site/).
- **Clean up a hacked site** properly and close the way in (some hosts will suspend the account instead).
- **Speed up a slow site** caused by heavy pages, images or too many plugins.
- **Make your changes:** new pages, forms, layout fixes.

If your host's support has ever replied "that's a plugin issue, please contact the developer", you've met the gap.

## What WordPress maintenance covers

Maintenance is the work on your site itself, every month:

- Updates to WordPress, your theme and your plugins, **tested before they go live**.
- Backups kept **off** your server, so a server problem doesn't take them too.
- Monitoring for downtime and hacks, so someone knows before your customers do.
- Small changes and fixes, and a short report on what was done.

My [WordPress maintenance tasks guide](/blog/what-wordpress-maintenance-includes/) walks through a month of it.

## Should you buy hosting and maintenance together?

**Bundle them if** you're unhappy with your host anyway, you want one bill and one company to call, and you don't mind moving your site to the provider's servers. Plans that include both start at around $99 a month.

**Keep them separate if** you like your host, your site is already set up well, or you want to be able to change maintenance provider without moving your site. Most maintenance providers, me included, work on whatever host you already use.

## What the two cost together

- **Hosting:** shared hosting starts at a few dollars a month; managed WordPress hosting costs more. Cheap plans are fine for brochure sites; stores and busy sites need more.
- **Maintenance:** published plans run roughly $59 to $300 a month for most small sites; I compared five in [the best WordPress maintenance services](/blog/best-wordpress-maintenance-services/).
- **Bundled:** from about $99 a month for both, rising with edits and support.

## Questions to ask your host before you rely on it

1. Do you test plugin updates, or just run them?
2. Where are backups stored, and how do I restore one myself?
3. What happens if my site is hacked: do you clean it, or suspend it?
4. If a plugin breaks my site, will you fix it or send me to a developer?
