## What did Tabby ask me to build?

Tabby is an AI accountant for small businesses and the self-employed. The team had finished designs in Figma and a tight deadline. They needed a marketing site that could launch in weeks and carry far more than pages: a tax calculator, a help center, payments and analytics.

## What did I build?

- The full site from the Figma designs, responsive on every screen size
- A custom tax calculator with dynamic logic
- Custom plugins for the AI demos and dynamic content
- WooCommerce with subscription payments
- A help center, blog and product updates on one content structure
- CRM, Mixpanel and Crisp Chat integrations
- Security audits and vulnerability patching

## How did the 2.0 relaunch keep Tabby's Google traffic?

When Tabby released version 2.0, I built the entire new website. The condition was simple: the new site had to keep the search visibility the old one had earned. If you're planning a redesign, that's the same worry as [redesigning a website without losing SEO](/blog/redesign-website-without-losing-seo/).

## What was the result?

- Launched on schedule
- Core Web Vitals above 90
- I still maintain and update the site today

## How did the Figma to WordPress build work?

- I built every page from the Figma file as drawn, then checked it at phone, tablet and desktop widths
- I wrote features specific to Tabby, the tax calculator and the AI demos, as plugins instead of forcing them out of general ones
- Content the team updates often (help articles, blog posts, product updates) shares one structure, so adding a page doesn't need a developer
- I carried the search settings from the old site into the new one before the 2.0 switch, so pages kept their addresses and titles

If you have designs waiting to be built, [how to hand off Figma designs that build cleanly](/blog/figma-to-wordpress-handoff/) covers what I need from you.

## What does the Tabby site look like today?

I read the live site page by page on 11 October 2026, and ran Google PageSpeed Insights on mobile.

**How big is it?** 302 pages in the sitemap:

- 164 blog articles
- 36 help center documents
- 26 pages of free financial tools
- 25 pages under the 1099 tax calculator
- 11 solution pages, one per kind of customer
- 5 product pages

**What do the pages do?**

- The 1099 tax calculator is a working form for income and deductions, not a static page
- A pricing page, a page written for accountants, a comparison page and a security page
- A public roadmap, user reviews, events, a partner program and an affiliate program

**How is it built?**

- WordPress with Elementor, on the Hello Elementor base theme
- Rank Math for search, WP Rocket as the one caching layer
- BetterDocs for the help center, Fluent Forms for forms, The Events Calendar for events

**What do search engines see?**

- SEO score 100, accessibility 96 and best practices 96 in PageSpeed Insights
- Structured data for the organisation, the website, the software product, its offer and its reviews
- Every one of the 39 images on the home page has alt text
